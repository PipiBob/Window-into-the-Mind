# Academic guided skimming

Read the whole paper, including the evidence needed to understand its claims. Reconstruct its reasoning as you would introduce it to a student. For a paper with several studies, understand what each adds and how it changes the interpretation of the others. Let the paper determine what matters.

Before marking text, ask what its paragraph is doing. A topic sentence can reveal the purpose. The useful definition, decision or result may come later. Follow the original document order and use links when the reader needs evidence from elsewhere. Prefer the earliest wording that carries the idea with enough context. Explicit questions, direct answers and reasons for consequential choices are often worth keeping.

Use the two levels of guidance where they help:

- **Semantic regions** name a meaningful part of the paper with a short label and a light outline. Choose boundaries that help a reader recognize the topic without boxing every paragraph. Regions can cover useful surrounding context.
- **Essential highlights** mark the smallest coherent original spans worth reading there. A selection may use separated spans. Keep the words that preserve the logic, comparison or qualification.

Read the highlighted path on its own. Add a source passage if its absence makes the reader guess a consequential relationship or misread a conclusion. Cut repetition and details that leave the reasoning intact. Explain a difficult result in one or two plain-language sentences only when it saves an interpretive step.

Use the [local resolver](format.md) and check the selected text and region boundaries in the original PDF. Keep explanations separate from the source wording. Report missing or ambiguous locations rather than guessing.
